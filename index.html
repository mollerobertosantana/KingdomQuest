    Field Guide to the Five Kingdoms

    Scientists sort living things into groups so they can study them. In the five-kingdom system, every organism goes into one kingdom based on three big questions: Do its cells have a nucleus? Is it one cell or many? How does it get its food?

    Compare the kingdoms

    A quick key for classifying any organism

    1. Does its cell have a nucleus? No: it is Monera.
    2. Is it a single cell, or a simple colony of cells that are all alike? Yes: it is Protista. Watch out for yeast, a single-celled member of Fungi.
    3. Is it green, making its own food by photosynthesis, with cellulose cell walls? Yes: it is Plantae.
    4. Does it absorb food from what it grows on, spread by spores and have chitin cell walls? Yes: it is Fungi.
    5. Does it eat its food and have cells with no cell walls? Yes: it is Animalia.

    The American ecologist Robert Whittaker proposed the five-kingdom system in 1969. Today many scientists also use three domains (Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya) and split life into more kingdoms, but the five kingdoms remain a clear way to begin.
